The voice keyboard that keeps your words on your iPhone.

brevva keys is a keyboard for iPhone with dictation built in. Tap the mic and talk: it takes out the ums, keeps only your correction, and types the words where you were typing. Speech, typing and tidying all happen on the phone. No account, nothing to sign in to.

How it works

Tap the mic, talk, tap Add.

  1. Tap the mic

    It sits at the right end of the bar above the keys. The keys turn into a small display that listens.

  2. Talk

    Your words appear as you speak. The last few, still settling, show in amber.

  3. Tap Add

    The words go in where you were typing, already tidied: filler out, punctuation in, your correction kept.

  4. A second tidy, still on the phone

    With Apple Intelligence turned on, Apple’s on-device model gives the words a careful second pass a moment later. Say a list and it comes out as a list.

Typing

Types like Apple’s keyboard, down to the key sizes.

Layout
The iOS layout and key sizes, measured from Apple’s own keyboard: keys 33.5 by 43 points, 6 points apart.
Autocorrect
Reads where each tap landed, fixes slips as you type, and takes a second look at the sentence when you pause.
Space bar
When a word looks finished, a tap that lands just above the space bar counts as a space.
Undo
Press delete once to put back what you typed. Tap the word in the bar and it will not make that change again.
Swipe
Slide from letter to letter. The shape is read on the phone.
Your shortcuts
Uses the text replacements you already set up in iPhone Settings.
Cursor
Hold the space bar and slide to move the cursor.

Your words

It learns your words, and leaves your phrases alone.

The only downloads

  • The first time you open the app, it downloads its voice files (about 600 MB) from Hugging Face, which hosts the speech model. Like any download, that shows your IP address to Hugging Face. Nothing you say or type is part of it.
  • If Apple’s US English dictation model is not on your iPhone yet, the app asks iOS to fetch it from Apple.
  • Nothing is uploaded. No analytics, no ads, no tracking, no account, no server of ours.

Why it asks for Full Access

iOS warns that a keyboard with Full Access could send what you type over the network. brevva keys uses it for two things: the microphone, and the folder it shares with its app. The keyboard has no code that sends anything over the network.

Typing works with Full Access off. Dictation and learning need it on.

Before you install

Who it suits, and who should wait.

YesIt suits you if

  • You write in English on an iPhone with iOS 26.2 or later.
  • You want dictation that tidies itself without a server.
  • Autocorrect keeps changing names or phrases you meant.
  • You would like to start talking from the Action Button or Siri.

Not yetWait if

  • You type mostly in another language. Typing and dictation are US English only for now.
  • You want it on iPad. It is an iPhone keyboard.
  • You want your dictionary on more than one device. It stays on the iPhone it learned on.
  • You cannot spare about 600 MB, or a Wi-Fi connection, the first time you open it.
  • You will not turn on Full Access. Typing works without it; dictation does not.

The second tidy needs an iPhone with Apple Intelligence turned on. Without it, the rules still tidy every dictation.

After you install

Setting it up.

  1. Open brevva keys

    It downloads its voice files once (about 600 MB) and asks to use the microphone and speech recognition. Stay on Wi-Fi with the app open until it says ready.

  2. Add the keyboard

    Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › Add New Keyboard, then pick brevva keys.

  3. Allow Full Access

    Tap brevva keys in that list and turn on Allow Full Access, for the mic and for learning your words.

  4. Switch to it

    In any app, hold the globe key and choose brevva keys.
