Turn on the keyboard

  1. Open the brevva keys app once, and allow the microphone and speech recognition when it asks.
  2. Open Settings, then General › Keyboard › Keyboards › Add New Keyboard, and choose brevva keys.
  3. Tap brevva keys in that list and turn on Allow Full Access.
  4. In any app, hold the globe key below the keys and pick brevva keys.

Why Full Access

iOS shows a warning when you turn it on, because a keyboard with Full Access could send what you type over the network. brevva keys uses it for three things, all on the phone:

  • the microphone you allowed for the brevva keys app, so the keyboard can listen;
  • a folder the app and keyboard share, where it keeps the words it learns;
  • the clipboard, so your recent copies can be offered again in the bar above the keys.

The keyboard makes no network connections of its own. Typing works with Full Access off; dictation, learning your words and recent copies need it. More in the privacy policy.

The first download

The first time you open the app, it downloads its voice files once (about 600 MB). Use Wi-Fi and keep the app open: it keeps the screen awake while it works, and it can look still for a while when it unpacks the files at the end. That is normal.

If it says it stopped part-way, tap to pick up where it left off. Finished files are kept, so it does not start over.

Use the mic

Version 2.1: in the keyboard

Tap the amber mic on the brevva keys keyboard. The keys turn into a small display that listens, and your words appear as you speak. Tap Add to put them where you were typing, or cancel to throw them away. It listens for up to three minutes at a time.

The globe and microphone in the strip below the keys belong to iOS. That microphone starts Apple’s own dictation, not brevva keys.

If your iPhone has Apple Intelligence turned on, the words you added are swapped a moment later for a second, more careful tidy, done on the phone. If you have typed on since, it leaves them as they are.

Version 2.0, or when the keyboard cannot listen

Tap the mic and brevva keys opens its own app to listen. Talk, tap to finish, then go back to where you were typing: your words are already there. They are also on your clipboard, so you can paste them anywhere.

From the Action Button or Siri

Say “Dictate with brevva keys” to Siri. For the Action Button: in Settings tap Action Button, swipe to Shortcut, tap Choose a Shortcut, and pick brevva keys › Dictate with brevva keys. Press and hold the Action Button to start talking.

Not sure which version you have? It is at the bottom of the app’s Settings.

Typing and autocorrect

  • Put back a word it changed: press delete once straight after. Or tap the word in the bar, and it will not make that change again.
  • Swipe: slide from letter to letter and lift. Pick another word from the bar if it guessed wrong.
  • Move the cursor: hold the space bar and slide.
  • Your shortcuts: text replacements from Settings › General › Keyboard › Text Replacement work here too.
  • Recent copies: tap the clipboard button at the left end of the bar to see your last ten copied snippets, and tap one to type it.
  • Turn things off: autocorrect, predictive text, swipe, auto-capitalisation, the full stop shortcut, smart punctuation, caps lock, key popups and haptics each have a switch in the app’s Settings.

Delete learned words

Open the brevva keys app, then Settings › Your words.

  • Your dictionary lists every word it has learned. Swipe one to the left to delete it.
  • Delete saved words clears what it learned from your typing, the corrections it remembers and how you tap.
  • Forget how you tap clears only where your thumbs land on the keys.
  • Save words you type off stops the keyboard learning anything new from your typing.

A known problem in versions 2.0 and 2.1: words learned from dictation can come back after you delete them, the next time the app learns from a dictation. Deleting the app removes everything it learned, for good.

Recent copies are not in Your dictionary. Newer copies push older ones out, and deleting the app clears them. The full list of what it keeps.

Something is not working

The mic key says to turn on Full Access

Turn on Allow Full Access for brevva keys in Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards › brevva keys.

The mic opened the brevva keys app instead of listening in the keyboard

In 2.1 the keyboard listens by itself when it can. It hands over to the app when:

  • Full Access is off, or the app has not been given the microphone yet. Open the app once and allow it.
  • Apple’s dictation model for US English is not on the iPhone yet. Open the app on Wi-Fi; it asks iOS to fetch it.
  • The iPhone is short of memory. Closing a few apps helps.

Either way your words still arrive. In 2.0, the app always does the listening.

Dictated words did not appear

Go back to the app you were typing in and tap the text box once. If they still do not appear, they are on your clipboard: paste them.

Autocorrect changed a word I meant

Press delete once straight after to put back what you typed, or tap the word in the bar so it never makes that change again. Words you keep typing are learned and left alone.

Dictation spells a name wrong

Dictation uses Apple’s speech model, which can spell a rare name its own way. Type the name with the keyboard five times and it is learned as one of your words, so autocorrect leaves it alone. Arabic greetings are written as In sha Allah, Asalam Alaikum and Alhamdulillah whichever way the speech model heard them.

The voice files download will not finish

Check you are on Wi-Fi with room for about 600 MB, keep the app open, and tap to try again. It carries on from where it stopped.

brevva keys is missing from the globe key

Add it again in Settings › General › Keyboard › Keyboards. Some apps and password boxes always switch to Apple’s keyboard; that is iOS, and brevva keys comes back when you leave them.

What it does not do yet

  • Typing and dictation are US English only.
  • It is an iPhone keyboard; there is no iPad version.
  • Your dictionary stays on the iPhone it learned on. It does not sync.
  • The second tidy needs an iPhone with Apple Intelligence turned on.
