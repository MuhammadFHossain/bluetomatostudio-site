brevva keys privacy policy

Last updated 21 November 2026. Covers the brevva keys app and its keyboard for iPhone, versions 2.0 and 2.1. The app used to be called brevva notes.

In short

  • What you type and what you say is handled on your iPhone. We do not collect it, see it or share it.
  • There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no ads and no server of ours.
  • The app downloads its voice files once, from Hugging Face. Nothing you say or type is sent.
  • Full Access is used for the microphone and for a folder the app and keyboard share on the phone. Nothing else.

Who we are

brevva keys is made by Blue Tomato Studio LLC. On the App Store the developer is listed as Muhammad Hossain. You can reach us at contact@bluetomatostudio.com.

What you type

The keyboard reads the keys you tap and the shapes you swipe so it can show the letter, suggest words and fix slips. All of that happens on your iPhone. The keyboard contains no code that sends anything over the network.

To get better at your typing, it remembers some things on your iPhone: the words you keep, corrections you undo, which words tend to follow which, and where your thumbs tend to land on each key. The full list is below.

It also keeps your last ten copied snippets (each under 500 characters) so you can paste them again from the bar above the keys. It reads the clipboard only when something new has been copied.

What you say

In the keyboard (version 2.1)

When you tap the mic, the keyboard listens through iOS, using the microphone permission you gave the brevva keys app. The sound goes straight to Apple’s dictation model on your iPhone and is turned into text as you speak. It is not saved to a file and it is not sent anywhere.

In the app (version 2.0, and 2.1 when the keyboard cannot listen)

The mic key opens the brevva keys app. Your voice is recorded to a temporary file on your iPhone, turned into text by a speech model on your iPhone, and the recording is deleted. To get the words back to where you were typing, the app puts them in the folder it shares with the keyboard and on your clipboard. If you use Universal Clipboard, iOS may make that clipboard available on your other Apple devices, as it does for anything you copy.

The app learns words you say often, such as names, so it can spell them right next time. That list stays on your iPhone and you can see it under Your dictionary in the app.

Tidying your words

Dictated text is first tidied by brevva’s own rules on your iPhone: filler words out, repeats and false starts removed, your correction kept. If your iPhone has Apple Intelligence turned on, Apple’s on-device language model then gives it a second tidy. brevva keys only uses Apple’s on-device model for this. It does not use Private Cloud Compute or any other online service.

What stays on your iPhone

These are kept in the app’s storage and in an App Group, a folder on your iPhone that only the brevva keys app and its keyboard can open. None of it is sent anywhere.

Allow Full Access

iOS asks you to turn on Allow Full Access for some keyboards, and warns that a keyboard with Full Access can send what you type over the network. brevva keys does not use it that way. It uses Full Access for two things:

  • The microphone, so the keyboard can listen when you tap the mic (version 2.1).
  • The shared folder, so the words it learns are kept, and your dictated words and learned names can pass between the app and the keyboard.

The keyboard types without Full Access. Dictation and learning need it.

Every network connection

This is the complete list of times brevva keys uses the internet.

  • Voice files, the first time you open the app. The app downloads its speech model (about 600 MB) from Hugging Face, which hosts it. Like any download, the request shows your IP address to Hugging Face, under its privacy policy. Nothing you say or type is part of it. If the files are later missing, it downloads them again.
  • Apple’s dictation model, if it is not on your iPhone yet. The app asks iOS to fetch Apple’s US English dictation model. iOS downloads it from Apple.
  • Links you tap. “Rate brevva keys” opens the App Store. Emailing us opens your mail app.

There is nothing else: no analytics, no crash-reporting service, no advertising, no tracking and no server of ours.

What we collect

Nothing. We cannot see what you type or say, or what the app has learned. If you have turned on Share With App Developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may pass us anonymous crash reports and usage figures for the app; that is Apple’s system, and brevva keys adds nothing to it. If you email us, we see only what you send.

Deleting what it learned

Open brevva keys, then Settings, Your words. Delete saved words clears what the keyboard learned from your typing, and Forget how you tap clears where your thumbs land. Single words can be swiped away in Your dictionary. Deleting the app deletes everything on this page from your iPhone.

Children

brevva keys is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone.
