Privacy

brevva keys privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026. Covers the brevva keys app and its keyboard for iPhone, versions 2.0 and 2.1.

In short

  • What you type and what you say is handled on your iPhone. We do not collect it, see it or share it.
  • There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no tracking and no server of ours.
  • The app downloads its voice files once, from Hugging Face. Nothing you say or type is sent.
  • Full Access is used for the microphone, a folder the app and keyboard share on the phone, and your recent copies. Nothing else.

Who we are

brevva keys is made by Blue Tomato Studio LLC. On the App Store the developer is listed as Muhammad Hossain. You can reach us at contact@bluetomatostudio.com.

What you type

The keyboard reads the keys you tap and the shapes you swipe so it can show the letter, suggest words and fix slips. All of that happens on your iPhone. The keyboard makes no network connections of its own.

To get better at your typing, it remembers some things on your iPhone: the words you keep, corrections you undo, which words tend to follow which, and where your thumbs tend to land on each key. The full list is below.

With Full Access on, it also keeps your last ten copied snippets so you can paste them again from the bar above the keys. It looks at the clipboard when the keyboard opens, and only if something new was copied. It keeps copied text of 500 characters or less. From version 2.1 it skips anything the copying app marks as a password or a one-time code (password managers mark them), and Clear at the end of the row empties the list. In version 2.0 it can keep a code or a password you copied, so it is worth knowing it is there. It stays on your iPhone.

What you say

In the keyboard (version 2.1)

When you tap the mic, the keyboard listens through WebKit, the part of iOS that shows web pages, under the microphone permission you gave the brevva keys app. The page it uses is built into the keyboard and loads nothing from the internet. The sound goes straight to Apple’s dictation model on your iPhone and is turned into text as you speak. It is not saved to a file and it is not sent anywhere.

If Apple’s dictation model is not on your iPhone, the keyboard does not fall back to a server. It hands over to the app, as described next.

In the app (version 2.0, and 2.1 when the keyboard cannot listen)

The mic key opens the brevva keys app. The Action Button and Siri start here too. Your voice is recorded to a temporary file on your iPhone. While you talk, Apple’s speech recognition shows the words on screen, set to work on the iPhone only. brevva’s own speech model, also on the iPhone, then turns the recording into the final text, and the recording is deleted.

To get the words back to where you were typing, the app puts them in the folder it shares with the keyboard and on your clipboard. If you use Universal Clipboard, iOS may make that clipboard available on your other Apple devices, as it does for anything you copy.

If you start it by asking Siri, Siri handles that request the way it handles any other. brevva keys starts listening after that.

Words you dictate

The app counts the words you dictate (those longer than three letters) so it can suggest them while you type. Words you dictate in the keyboard wait in the shared folder, up to the last 50 dictations, until you next open the app; it counts them and clears them. The counts stay on your iPhone and are listed under Your dictionary in the app. This counting happens even when Save words you type is off.

Tidying your words

Dictated text is first tidied by brevva’s own rules on your iPhone: filler words out, repeats and false starts removed, your correction kept. If your iPhone has Apple Intelligence turned on, Apple’s on-device language model then gives it a second tidy. The model is given only the text and a short list of words to spell right. brevva keys only uses Apple’s on-device model for this. It does not use Private Cloud Compute or any other online service.

What stays on your iPhone

These are kept in the app’s storage and in an App Group, a folder on your iPhone that only the brevva keys app and its keyboard can open. None of it is sent anywhere by brevva keys. Like other apps’ data, iOS can include it in your iPhone’s own backups.

Turning off Save words you type in the app’s settings stops the keyboard saving anything new about your typing.

The app also has developer tools, hidden until you tap the version number in Settings five times. One of them, Log taps for replay, is off unless you turn it on. Allow Full Access

iOS asks you to turn on Allow Full Access for some keyboards, and warns that a keyboard with Full Access can send what you type over the network. brevva keys does not use it that way. It uses Full Access for three things:

  • The microphone, so the keyboard can listen when you tap the mic (version 2.1).
  • The shared folder, so the words it learns are kept, and your settings and dictated words can pass between the app and the keyboard.
  • The clipboard, so new copies can be offered again in the bar above the keys.

The keyboard types without Full Access. Dictation, learning your words and recent copies need it.

Every network connection

This is the complete list of times brevva keys uses the internet.

  • Voice files, the first time you open the app. The app downloads its speech model (about 600 MB) from Hugging Face, which hosts it. Like any download, the request shows your IP address to Hugging Face, under its privacy policy. Nothing you say or type is part of it. If the files are later missing, it downloads them again.
  • Apple’s dictation model, if it is not on your iPhone yet. The app asks iOS to fetch Apple’s US English dictation model. iOS downloads it from Apple.
  • Links you tap. “Rate brevva keys” opens the App Store. Emailing us opens your mail app.

There is nothing else: no analytics, no crash-reporting service, no tracking and no server of ours. The keyboard itself makes no network connections.

What we collect

Nothing. We cannot see what you type or say, or what the app has learned. If you have turned on Share With App Developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may pass us anonymous crash reports and usage figures for the app; that is Apple’s system, and brevva keys adds nothing to it. If you email us, we see only what you send.

Deleting what it learned

Open brevva keys, then Settings, Your words. Delete saved words clears what the keyboard learned from your typing, the corrections it remembers and how you tap. Forget how you tap clears only where your thumbs land. Single words can be swiped away in Your dictionary.

A known problem in versions 2.0 and 2.1: words learned from dictation can come back after you delete them inside the app, the next time the app learns from a dictation. Deleting the app removes them for good. We will change this page when that is fixed.

Deleting the app deletes everything on this page from your iPhone.

Children

brevva keys is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone.
